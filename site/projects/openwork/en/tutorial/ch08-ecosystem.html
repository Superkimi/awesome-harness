    2  title: "Connectors, skills, and plugins"
    3  description: "Use connectors for systems and plugins for the skills that make a system usable"
    4  ---
    5
    6  OpenWork gives agents three building blocks. The connector reaches the system; the skill is the playbook; the plugin is the bundle.
    7
    8  ## Choose the right unit
    9
   10  | Use | Means | Reach for it when |
   11  | --- | --- | --- |
   12  | Connector | How OpenWork reaches mail, calendar, CRM, or your ticketing system. | The agent needs to act in that system after you connect it once. |
   13  | Skill | Written instructions the agent loads when a task matches. | One repeatable way of doing work needs a playbook. |
   14  | Plugin | A bundle OpenWork can install or distribute as one thing. | Several related skills should travel together. |
   15
   16  MCP is the open standard underneath many connectors, but day to day you can think in terms of apps, connectors, skills, and plugins.
   17
   18  ## Connect the system first
   19
   20  Use connectors for the apps themselves. In the desktop app, start in `Settings` > `OpenWork Connect`; for the click-by-click flow, see [Connect your services](/start-here/connect-your-stack/connect-services).
   21
   22  Everything your agent can use is listed in `Settings` > `Extensions`, under `Available apps` and `Your apps`. Use `Add Custom App` to connect something we do not list yet.
   23
   24  ## Add skills when the connector is thin
   25
   26  A connector may reach your ticketing system but still be awkward: an incident may need exact fields, a priority change may need a safe sequence, or one ticket action may behave badly.
   27
   28  Write skills for those system rules:
   29
   30  - how to file an incident with the right fields;
   31  - how to update a customer note without losing context;
   32  - how to work around an unreliable ticket action.
   33
   34  These skills describe the system, not one person's style, so everyone who uses that system needs them.
